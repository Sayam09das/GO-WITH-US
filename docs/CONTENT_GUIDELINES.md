# GO WITH US — Content Guidelines

This document defines the tone of voice, copywriting standards, editorial guidelines, metadata specifications, and content rules for **GO WITH US**.

---

## 1. Purpose

The content system ensures all written text throughout GO WITH US reflects a premium, editorial, human, and travel-focused experience. Content must inspire travel exploration and facilitate day-by-day trip planning without sounding like generic travel advertising or an aggressive booking portal.

---

## 2. Brand Voice

GO WITH US speaks with a distinct brand voice:

* **Warm**: Welcoming travelers to explore destinations without pretension.
* **Confident**: Clear, assured statements using concise language.
* **Curious**: Inspiring discovery of unique places, local experiences, and hidden gems.
* **Human**: Writing naturally, conversationally, and empathetically.
* **Refined**: Polished editorial composition that respects the user's attention.
* **Practical**: Delivering accurate, scan-friendly, and actionable travel information.

---

## 3. Content Personality

The core personality is:

> **Curious + Calm + Confident + Editorial + Human**

### Anti-Tone Examples
GO WITH US must **NEVER** sound like:
* A high-pressure, transactional hotel booking marketplace (*"ONLY 1 ROOM LEFT AT THIS PRICE!"*).
* Corporate corporate-speak or robotic database dumps.
* Overly dramatic or exaggerated travel blogs (*"Literally the most mind-blowing paradise on Earth!"*).
* Generic AI chatbot outputs filled with repetitive fluff phrases.

---

## 4. Core Brand Message

### Tagline & Mission
> **Discover places. Save what inspires you. Build your trip. Go with us.**

### User Journey Progression
```text
Discover ──► Explore ──► Save ──► Plan ──► Organize ──► Travel ──► Remember
```

---

## 5. Writing Style

* **Use Short, Active Sentences**: Prefer direct, powerful verbs (*"Discover Kyoto's historic temples"* over *"You can discover many historic temples in Kyoto"*).
* **Specific Over Generic**: Describe distinct characteristics of destinations and stays rather than vague praise.
* **Concise Copy**: Keep supporting paragraphs to 1–2 crisp sentences.
* **No Artificial Urgency**: Never invent fake countdowns or scarcity pressure.
* **No Exclamation Mark Overuse**: Rely on strong nouns and verbs rather than punctuation to build excitement.

---

## 6. Headlines

Headlines should be short, evocative, scan-friendly, and visually grounded:

* *"Find somewhere worth going."*
* *"Your next place starts here."*
* *"Go where curiosity takes you."*
* *"Places you'll want to remember."*

---

## 7. Homepage Hero Copy

The hero communicates the core value proposition instantly without wall-of-text fatigue:

```text
[ Eyebrow ]: TRAVEL DISCOVERY & TRIP PLANNING
[ Headline ]: Discover places. Build your trip.
[ Description ]: Explore curated destinations, bookmark stays and experiences, and organize personalized day-by-day itineraries.
[ Search Bar Action ]: "Where do you want to go?"
```

---

## 8. Section Headings

Use purposeful section headings that reflect traveler intent:

* *Explore destinations*
* *Places worth discovering*
* *Stay somewhere memorable*
* *Things to do along the way*
* *Travel by feeling*
* *Inspiration for your next trip*

Avoid repetitive, generic headers such as *"Best Destinations"*, *"Top Hotels"*, or *"Popular Items"*.

---

## 9. Supporting Copy

Supporting descriptions clarify why a section exists in 1–2 sentences. Avoid filler text.

* *Good*: "From quiet mountain retreats to vibrant coastal cities, explore destinations tailored to your travel style."
* *Bad*: "Check out these incredible destinations that are super amazing and will change your life forever!"

---

## 10. Calls to Action (CTAs)

Use direct, action-oriented button copy:

* `Explore destinations`
* `Discover stays`
* `Explore experiences`
* `Save place`
* `Add to trip`
* `Create a trip`
* `Open itinerary`
* `View details`

Avoid vague CTAs such as *"Click here"*, *"Learn more"*, or *"Submit"*.

---

## 11. Navigation Labels

Maintain strict terminology consistency across the entire application:

* **Destinations**: Locations and cities.
* **Stays**: Accommodations and lodging.
* **Experiences**: Tours, activities, and attractions.
* **Trips**: User trip containers and itineraries.
* **Saved**: Bookmarked items collection.

Do not substitute synonyms dynamically (e.g. switching between *Trips*, *Journeys*, and *Adventures*).

---

## 12. Destination Content

Destination copy should paint a vivid, authentic picture:

* **Include**: Location title, country, region, short overview, key highlights, best time to visit, climate summary, and curated stays/experiences.
* **Avoid**: Generic praise like *"A breathtaking paradise of heavenly beauty."*
* **Prefer**: Specific descriptions like *"A coastal sanctuary known for sea-cliff hiking, morning fish markets, and historic wooden villas."*

---

## 13. Destination Metadata

Keep destination metadata concise and factual:
* **Country & Region**: "Japan • Kansai Region"
* **Travel Style Tags**: *Culture*, *Culinary*, *Heritage*
* **Recommended Stay**: "3–5 Days"
* **Budget Category**: Moderate ($$)

Never invent fake travel statistics or false information.

---

## 14. Stay Content

Stay descriptions should highlight property character and actual amenities:
* **Title**: Property Name (e.g. *Koto Heritage Villa*)
* **Type**: Property Type (*Boutique Hotel*, *Villa*, *Eco-Lodge*, *Apartment*)
* **Location**: Specific neighborhood context (*Gion District, Kyoto*)
* **Description**: Accurate 2-sentence breakdown of rooms, design, and atmosphere.

---

## 15. Experience Content

Experience copy explains clearly:
1. What the traveler will do.
2. Where it occurs and how long it lasts.
3. Included highlights and necessary requirements.

*Example*: "A 3-hour guided evening walking tour exploring Kyoto's historic Gion district, focusing on traditional tea houses and local culinary specialties."

---

## 16. Search Content

* **Input Placeholder**: *"Where do you want to go?"*
* **No-Results Message**: *"Nothing matched that search. Try searching for a different destination or explore popular places below."*
* **Recovery Action**: Provide a single-click `[ Clear Filters ]` or `[ View Popular Destinations ]` button.

---

## 17. Filter Labels

Use intuitive, everyday travel terms:
* *Location*
* *Category*
* *Travel Style*
* *Price Tier* ($ / $$ / $$$)
* *Rating* (4.0+, 4.5+)
* *Duration*
* *Amenities*

---

## 18. Saved Content

* **Header**: *"Saved Places"*
* **Subtitle**: *"Keep the places you want to come back to."*
* **Empty State**: *"Nothing saved yet. Explore destinations, stays, and experiences and save the ones you want to remember."*

---

## 19. Trip Content

Use personal travel language rather than project management terms:

* **Use**: *Trip Name*, *Destination*, *Travel Dates*, *Day-by-Day Itinerary*, *Activity Notes*.
* **Do NOT Use**: *Project*, *Task Board*, *Backlog*, *Sprint*, *Status Report*.

---

## 20. Itinerary Content

Keep itinerary item titles and notes clean and scannable:
* **Day Labels**: *Day 1*, *Day 2*, *Day 3* (accompanied by calendar date).
* **Time Blocks**: *09:00 AM — Morning Coffee & Bakery Visit*, *02:00 PM — Hotel Check-in*.

---

## 21. Account Content

Use clean, friendly settings headers: *Profile*, *Preferences*, *Travel History*, *Settings*, *Security*.

---

## 22. Authentication Content

### Sign In
* **Header**: *"Welcome back."*
* **Subtitle**: *"Sign in to access your saved places and trip itineraries."*

### Sign Up
* **Header**: *"Plan somewhere you'll love."*
* **Subtitle**: *"Create an account to save places and build day-by-day trips."*

---

## 23. Error Messages

Errors clearly explain **What happened + What action to take next**:

* *Good*: "We couldn't load this destination right now. Check your network connection and try again." `[ Retry ]`
* *Bad*: "Error 500 Internal Server Exception: Connection refused."

---

## 24. Loading Content

Keep loading messages simple:
* *Loading destinations…*
* *Finding stays…*
* *Loading your itinerary…*

---

## 25. Empty States

Structure every empty state with 3 items:
1. Short Title (*"No trips created yet"*)
2. Reassuring Explanation (*"Start planning your next getaway by exploring destinations."*)
3. Primary CTA Button (`[ Explore Destinations ]`)

---

## 26. Reviews & Ratings

* Display rating averages (e.g. *4.8 / 5.0*) alongside review counts (*124 Reviews*).
* Never fabricate fake guest reviews or artificially inflate ratings.

---

## 27. Notifications

Keep alerts short and actionable:
* *"Your upcoming trip to Kyoto starts in 3 days."*
* *"A new experience was saved to your collection."*

---

## 28. Personalization

Use transparent, contextual phrasing for recommendations:
* *"Based on your saved places"*
* *"Because you like coastal escapes"*

---

## 29. Editorial Travel Content

Format travel stories like digital magazine articles:
1. Inspiring Title & Subtitle.
2. High-impact photography.
3. Engaging 2-paragraph travel narrative.
4. Curated list of linked stays and experiences.
5. Primary CTA (`[ Build a Trip Around This ]`).

---

## 30. Content Accuracy

Never invent or hallucinate:
* Prices, hotel amenities, opening hours, transport schedules, distances, or ratings.
* Omit missing information or indicate "Information upon request" rather than guessing.

---

## 31. Content Source Strategy

* **Development Phase**: Use realistic, internally consistent local fixtures (`data/fixtures/*.ts`).
* **Production Phase**: Replaced by backend REST API responses matching TypeScript interfaces.

---

## 32. SEO Content

Technical SEO (metadata, canonical URLs, robots, sitemap, JSON-LD) is defined in **`docs/SEO.md`**. This section covers editorial copy only.

* **Titles**: Dynamic, informative format (`"Kyoto Travel Guide | GO WITH US"`).
* **Descriptions**: Concise 140–160 character summaries with natural language — never keyword stuffing.

---

## 33. Image Alt Text

Alt text describes the actual visual subject:
* *Good*: `"Sunrise over wooden tea houses in Kyoto's Gion district"`
* *Bad*: `"travel image"` or `"photo123.jpg"`

---

## 34. Content Length Guidelines

* **Navigation**: 1–2 words
* **Buttons**: 1–4 words
* **Section Headings**: 2–6 words
* **Card Descriptions**: 1–2 short sentences
* **Destination Overviews**: 2–3 concise paragraphs

---

## 35. Localization Readiness

Store user-facing strings cleanly in central dictionary files to facilitate future i18n localization without component refactoring.

---

## 36. Content Consistency Rule

```text
Destination ──► always Destination
Stay        ──► always Stay
Experience  ──► always Experience
Trip        ──► always Trip
Itinerary   ──► always Itinerary
Saved       ──► always Saved
```

---

## 37. Content + Visual Design

Text works hand-in-hand with typography and whitespace. Never crowd cards with dense text walls; let imagery and composition tell part of the story.

---

## 38. Content + Animation

Information must remain 100% legible and complete even if animations are disabled or failing.

---

## 39. Content Anti-Patterns

* **NO** transactional booking urgency.
* **NO** fake reviews, ratings, or prices.
* **NO** corporate productivity jargon (*"Sprints"*, *"Workspaces"*).
* **NO** keyword stuffing or excessive exclamation marks.

---

## 40. Content Quality Checklist

Before committing text, verify:
- [x] Is it clear, human, and concise?
- [x] Does it avoid fake sales urgency?
- [x] Are CTAs direct and action-oriented?
- [x] Is terminology consistent with standard domain names?
- [x] Are factual details supported by real or fixture data?

---

## 41. Final Content Direction

> **GO WITH US content should feel like a knowledgeable traveler helping you discover somewhere worth going—not a booking website trying to sell you something.**
